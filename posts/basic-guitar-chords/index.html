New here? Start practising in five steps
  1. Tune first: press Tuner above, then tap a string for its reference tone or let the microphone listen.
  2. Learn the shapes: tap a card to hear it, then fret it and strum to compare. Squares mark the root. Start with Em → Am → C → G → D.
  3. Stuck on barres: F, Bm and Fm cards switch to an Easy voicing; the 1–6–4–5 and royal road loops use Fmaj7 instead of F.
  4. Practise changes: press ▶ Play on a progression; pick another Key and it works out the capo and shapes. Lower the BPM and tick Loop. Tick Click only to strum yourself.
  5. Hands on the guitar: after the first play, Space (or a page-turner pedal set to Space) stops and restarts. The screen stays awake while playing.

maj · m · 7 · sus · add9 · /: chord symbols at a glance

1 = root; intervals refer to its major scale. ♭ = one semitone lower. The feel depends on rhythm, arrangement and context.

SymbolIntervalsDifference / feel
maj C1 · 3 · 5Major triad; 3 is four semitones above the root. Often bright. C usually omits “maj”.
m Am1 · ♭3 · 5Lower the third by one semitone (three above the root). Often darker.
7 G71 · 3 · 5 · ♭7Major triad plus minor seventh (10 semitones). Tension, often resolving home; also a blues sound.
maj7 Cmaj71 · 3 · 5 · 7Add a major seventh (11 semitones), one semitone higher than ♭7. Often mellow or dreamy.
m7 Am71 · ♭3 · 5 · ♭7Minor triad plus minor seventh. Often mellow and understated.
sus2 Dsus21 · 2 · 5Replace the third with a second (two semitones). No major/minor third; often open or floating.
sus4 Asus41 · 4 · 5Replace the third with a fourth (five semitones). Often suspended; 4 can resolve to 3. “sus” usually means sus4.
add9 Cadd91 · 3 · 5 · 9Keep the third and add a ninth (the 2 an octave up). Unlike sus2 it keeps its major/minor colour; common in pop.
5 E51 · 5Power chord: root and fifth, no third. Stays clear under distortion; a rock staple.
/ G/BChord / bassChord over a bass note: G/B is G with B lowest. It lets the bass step, as in the Canon’s C → G/B → Am.

Remember: 7 ≠ maj7. sus replaces the third; keeping it and adding 2 or 4 gives add2 / add4. Uppercase Roman numerals mean major, lowercase mean minor; progression degrees refer to the key’s tonic.
