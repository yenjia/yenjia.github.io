maj · m · 7 · sus: chord symbols at a glance

1 = root; intervals refer to its major scale. ♭ = one semitone lower. The feel depends on rhythm, arrangement and context.

SymbolIntervalsDifference / feel
maj C1 · 3 · 5Major triad; 3 is four semitones above the root. Often bright. C usually omits “maj”.
m Am1 · ♭3 · 5Lower the third by one semitone (three above the root). Often darker.
7 G71 · 3 · 5 · ♭7Major triad plus minor seventh (10 semitones). Tension, often resolving home; also a blues sound.
maj7 Cmaj71 · 3 · 5 · 7Add a major seventh (11 semitones), one semitone higher than ♭7. Often mellow or dreamy.
m7 Am71 · ♭3 · 5 · ♭7Minor triad plus minor seventh. Often mellow and understated.
sus2 Dsus21 · 2 · 5Replace the third with a second (two semitones). No major/minor third; often open or floating.
sus4 Asus41 · 4 · 5Replace the third with a fourth (five semitones). Often suspended; 4 can resolve to 3. “sus” usually means sus4.

Remember: 7 ≠ maj7. sus replaces the third; keeping it and adding 2 or 4 gives add2 / add4. Uppercase Roman numerals mean major, lowercase mean minor; progression degrees refer to the key’s tonic.
